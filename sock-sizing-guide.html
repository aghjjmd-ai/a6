The Sock Sizing Guide

A sock that fits sits flat, stays up and never bunches. Getting there takes a size range, a height and a quick look at the heel.

How sock sizes work

Socks are knitted to stretch, so they are sold in size ranges rather than single sizes. A typical chart looks like this:

Sock sizeUS men’s shoeUS women’s shoeEU shoe
S4–65–735–38
M6.5–97.5–1039–42
L9.5–1210.5–1343–46
XL12.5–15—47–50

Ranges differ between makers, so always check the chart on the packet.

Between sizes?

If you are at the top of a range, or wearing thick socks, size up. If you are at the bottom of a range, or wearing thin dress socks, the smaller size usually sits more neatly. Use the Size Converter for a quick answer.

Sock heights

  • No-show: hidden inside low shoes.
  • Ankle: just covers the ankle bone; good with trainers.
  • Quarter: a little above the ankle.
  • Crew: mid-calf; the everyday classic.
  • Over-the-calf: stays up under formal trousers.
  • Knee-high: for tall boots and warmth.

The heel pocket test

Put a sock on and check where the heel shaping sits. It should cup your heel exactly. If it sits on the back of your ankle, the sock is too big; if it pulls under your heel, it is too small. A badly placed heel is the main cause of twisting and slipping.

Signs a sock doesn’t fit

  • Fabric bunches at the toe or under the arch.
  • The cuff leaves deep marks on your leg.
  • The heel twists to the side during the day.
  • The sock creeps down inside your shoe.

Fit changes over time

Hot washes shrink cotton and wool, and elastane loses spring with heat. If a favourite sock has started to feel tight or slack, it may simply have reached the end of its life.
