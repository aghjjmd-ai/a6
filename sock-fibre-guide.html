The Sock Fibre Guide

The fibre decides how warm, dry and long-lasting a sock will be. Here is what each one does well, and where it falls short.

The fibres at a glance

FibreBest atWatch out for
CottonSoft, breathable, everyday wearHolds sweat; slow to dry
Merino woolWarmth, moisture control, low odourNeeds gentle washing
Nylon / polyamideStrength at heel and toeRarely used alone
PolyesterQuick drying for sportCan hold odour
Bamboo viscoseVery soft, smooth feelLess durable than blends
ElastaneStretch and shapeWeakens with high heat

Why blends are usually best

Almost every good sock is a blend. A typical everyday sock might be around 75–80% cotton or merino for comfort, 15–20% nylon for strength and 2–5% elastane so it keeps its shape. A sock that is 100% of any single fibre rarely fits or wears as well.

Cotton: the everyday default

Cotton is comfortable and breathable in mild weather, which is why it fills most sock drawers. Its weakness is moisture: it soaks up sweat and holds it, which can lead to blisters on long walks. Combed or long-staple cotton feels smoother and pills less.

Merino: the all-rounder

Fine merino wool is not itchy like old-fashioned wool. It keeps feet warm in cold weather and comfortable in mild weather, moves sweat away from the skin and resists odour, so it suits hiking and travel. Wash it cool and dry it flat.

Synthetics for sport

Polyester and nylon dry quickly and hold their structure under impact, so running and gym socks rely on them. Look for mesh panels on top of the foot for ventilation and cushioning under the heel and ball.

Washing by fibre

  • Cotton: 30–40°C, tumble dry low is fine.
  • Merino: cold or wool cycle, inside out, dry flat.
  • Synthetic sport: 30°C, no fabric softener.
  • Bamboo viscose: cool and gentle; avoid wringing.
